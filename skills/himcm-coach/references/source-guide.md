# Source guide and cautions

This guide distills user-provided materials from one coaching conversation. It is not an exhaustive transcription, official rubric or proof that all source pages were parsed. Original PDFs remain with the user and are not redistributed. Identify the original source and page when a detailed claim is needed.

- COMAP *Consortium* 116 (2019), PDF pp. 10–16: 2018 HiMCM judges explain complete responses, justified assumptions, clear model development, final model, numerical results, sensitivity, limitations and citations; high-school mathematics can suffice. PDF pp. 18–29 include image-only exemplars that were not fully extracted. Any deadlines or limits are historical.
- COMAP *Consortium* 121 (2021), PDF pp. 9–29: pooled-test simulations, regression and residuals. PDF pp. 35 onward discuss 2021 IM²C, not HiMCM; some equations and images did not extract. Do not transfer its contest rules to HiMCM.
- COMAP *Consortium* 130 (2026), 232 pages: contains 2025 HiMCM results, judges' commentary, contest examples and separate MidMCM and teaching material. Much material was not fully read because parts were image-only or extracted as garbled text. Inspect actual pages before detailed use; distinguish the competitions and student examples from official rules.
- User's second lesson (23 PDF pages): AHP weights/consistency, TOPSIS, fuzzy evaluation, linear and integer optimization. Check the TOPSIS example: `argsort` yields an ordering, not the rank for each original row. Subjective weights, normalization and candidate sets can change ranking; perform sensitivity checks. Higher-level KKT and sophisticated optimization do not automatically fit the user's academic boundary.
- Third lesson (8 pages): OLS, regression assumptions, regularization and diagnostics. The house-price coefficients printed in prose do not match the five observations in its code; recompute. A high in-sample R² alone does not validate forecasting, and association does not prove causation.
- Fourth lesson (14 pages): t tests, ANOVA, chi-square, K-means and PCA. Its ANOVA example runs unadjusted pairwise t tests; use a suitable multiple-comparison procedure. Interpret PCA and clusters as exploratory, and report units, assumptions, sample sizes and uncertainty.
- Fifth lesson (12 pages): logistic regression, trees, classification metrics and cross-validation. Its example runs CV on all data including the held-out test set. Reserve test data and fit preprocessing inside training folds. Labels derived from clusters measure recovery of those clusters, not independent future outcomes.
- Sixth lesson (18 pages): time series, graph methods and Monte Carlo. The example graph creates an edge attribute named `里程(km)` but requests `weight='km'`, producing incorrect weighted analyses unless reconciled. With fixed marginal default probabilities and losses, changing dependence alone does not change expected loss; tail effects depend on model assumptions. A residual test's failure to reject does not prove whiteness or guarantee forecast quality. Weighted centrality must specify the same meaning and units as the weights.
- Seventh lesson (16 pages): summaries, result interpretation and graphical literacy. Its five-sentence framework, page allocation and fixed counts are suggestions, not official rules. Eta-squared of 0.235 does not mean almost all variation. A systematic prediction bias or correlation alone does not establish its cause; compare test coverage and sample size without overclaiming.

Method selection: evaluation needs transparent criteria and weights; optimization needs a defined objective and constraints; forecasting needs a realistic baseline and chronological holdout; inference needs sampling assumptions and appropriate error control. Always inspect current problem requirements and verify calculations instead of copying textbook example numbers.
